About

I am an incoming PhD student in Statistics and Data Science at the National University of Singapore (NUS), with a strong background in mathematics and statistics.

My research interests broadly lie in statistical methodology, particularly Bayesian inference, distribution theory, and related areas. I am interested in the development and study of statistical models, including their theoretical properties and applications to real-world data.

Alongside my research interests, I have a strong background in mathematics teaching, with experience teaching the Cambridge IGCSE and AS & A Level Mathematics curricula and in the assessment of mathematics examinations.

You can find more details about my background in my CV (PDF).

The topics below are my main research interests. I am also open to other areas of statistics, and I would be glad to learn new topics together with a future supervisor.

Research focus

My publications and talks come from one line of work: building new probability distributions and studying how well they describe real data. So far this has mostly been about lifetime and count data, using parametric inference to fit the models, and I am interested in extending it with Bayesian methods.

Keywords: Bayesian inference, distribution theory, parametric inference, statistical modelling
